What rodent control near Castle Rock actually looks like.

Castle Rock is an incorporated home-rule town and the county seat of Douglas County, the most populous municipality in the county, with its own town government and a mix of historic downtown and large newer subdivisions.

Every Castle Rock visit starts from our Littleton base, roughly 12.3 miles away, and follows the identical inspect, trap, seal order we use citywide. Because the town mixes older and newer buildings, the first question on a call is how old the house is, since that decides whether we start at the foundation and roofline or at the utility penetrations.

A county seat with a historic core and newer subdivisions.

Castle Rock's small historic downtown, named for the distinctive rock formation above it, sits alongside much newer subdivision development. That range means an inspection here has to consider both an older downtown building's settled gaps and a newer subdivision home's more specific, limited entry points.

Newer Castle Rock subdivisions tend to see activity concentrated around a single missed utility penetration, while the historic downtown core sees more of the general wear typical of century-old construction. The technician checks utility lines first in one and the foundation line first in the other.

What people in Castle Rock call about.

How treatment and sealing work here.

For newer Castle Rock homes, rodent exclusion and entry point sealing usually targets a specific utility penetration or vent. Garage and basement checks come up on nearly every Castle Rock call we take, since garage and basement rodent control applies regardless of how old the specific home is.
