Finding the travel routes first.

Trapping only works when the traps sit on an actual travel route, so this service always follows an inspection, even a short one on the same visit. Mice and rats both tend to hug walls, cabinet edges and foundation lines rather than cross open floor space, which means the routes are usually predictable once a technician has walked the property.

Droppings, grease marks and gnawed material mark the busiest routes. On a Littleton home, that is often a run along a basement sill plate, behind kitchen cabinets, or along a garage wall near a stored-goods shelf.

Choosing the right trap for the job.

Snap traps handle most indoor mouse activity. Enclosed bait stations go in spots where children or pets could otherwise reach a trap. For Norway rats, larger traps and stations are placed at burrow entrances and along yard fence lines rather than only indoors, since outdoor activity is common in Littleton yards near the South Platte River greenbelt and older hedgerows.

  • Snap traps along confirmed indoor travel routes.
  • Enclosed stations wherever children or pets have access.
  • Yard and burrow-entrance placement for outdoor rat activity.
A trap off the route catches nothing no matter how many you set.

Checking and clearing on a schedule.

Traps are not set once and forgotten. We schedule a return visit to clear what was caught, reset anything still active, and look for signs that activity has spread to a new area. That follow-up is where we also confirm whether rodent exclusion and entry point sealing is the next step or whether trapping alone cleared the problem.

When trapping alone is not enough.

Signs you likely need sealing too.

  • New droppings keep appearing after several clear trap checks.
  • Activity is heard in a different room than where it started.
  • A clear entry point, a gap, vent or crack, is visible from outside.

Trapping removes what is already inside. If the entry point stays open, a new mouse or rat eventually finds the same gap. We say so honestly rather than leaving a homeowner resetting traps indefinitely.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about rodent trapping and removal

How many visits does trapping usually take?

Most residential jobs clear in one to three visits, depending on how widespread the activity is and whether entry points also need sealing.

Are the traps safe around my dog or cat?

Enclosed bait stations go in any area pets can reach. Tell the technician about every pet in the home so placement is planned around them.

Do you remove the trapped animals, or do I have to?

We handle removal and disposal as part of the visit.
