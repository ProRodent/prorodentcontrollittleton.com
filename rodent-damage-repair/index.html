What rodent damage usually looks like.

Mice and rats need to gnaw to keep their teeth worn down, and that habit does real damage over time. Common results include chewed insulation that loses effectiveness, duct seams opened enough to leak conditioned air, gnawed soffit and fascia boards, and drywall or baseboard damage where an entry point was widened over weeks.

We document every piece of damage found during the inspection and cleanup, with photos, so the homeowner has a clear record rather than a vague description.

Insulation and duct repair.

  • Contaminated or chewed insulation removed and replaced where needed.
  • Duct seams and connections repaired to stop conditioned-air leaks.
  • Soffit and fascia damage patched or replaced to close the original gap.

Duct damage is worth catching early, since a leaking seam in an attic or crawl space can quietly raise energy bills long before anyone traces the cause back to a rodent problem.

Drywall and structural touch-ups.

Interior damage, a gnawed baseboard, a widened gap around a pipe, a chewed corner of drywall, gets repaired after the entry point behind it is sealed, so the fix actually holds. Repairing the cosmetic damage before closing the entry point just invites the same problem back.

Repair without sealing is just a nicer-looking version of the same open gap.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.

  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.

Questions about rodent damage repair

Will insurance cover rodent damage repair?

Coverage varies by policy. We can provide documented photos and a written scope to support a claim, but recommend checking with your insurer directly.

Do you repair the damage before or after sealing entry points?

After. Repairing damage before the related entry point is sealed risks the same problem recurring.

Can chewed wiring in the attic be a fire risk?

Yes, chewed electrical wiring anywhere in the home is a real risk. We flag it immediately and recommend a qualified electrician handle the repair.
