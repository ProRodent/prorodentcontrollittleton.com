Why Old Littleton needs rodent control

  • Smaller, older housing stock

    The original townsite grid was platted long before modern building codes, and many of the houses here have had multiple additions and repairs over the decades, each one a potential gap.

  • Narrow lots and close-set houses

    Houses sit closer together than in newer subdivisions, so activity on one property's foundation or woodpile can show up as a call from next door within weeks.

  • Mature trees and older landscaping

    Decades-old trees and overgrown fence lines common in this part of the city give rodents more outdoor cover than newer, more manicured yards typically have.

The original townsite grid.

Old Littleton covers the original townsite grid, where smaller, older houses sit on narrower lots than the subdivisions built later further south and east. Many of these homes have been added onto more than once, a bedroom here, a porch enclosure there, and each addition is a seam where the original structure meets newer framing, which is exactly where gaps tend to open up over time.

Foundations in this part of the city are older too, often poured or laid decades before current sealing standards, which means settling cracks are more common here than on newer construction.

Close-set lots and shared pressure.

Because houses in Old Littleton sit closer together than in newer developments, a woodpile or shed on one property can be the source of activity that shows up on neighboring lots. We ask about neighboring activity during the inspection here more than we might in a more spread-out subdivision, since it often points to where the actual source is.

  • Foundation seams from additions and repairs checked carefully.
  • Settling cracks in older poured or laid foundations.
  • Woodpiles, sheds and overgrown fence lines checked as outdoor cover.
A house that has been added onto three times has three times the seams to check.

Sealing homes with a longer history.

Where an addition meets the original structure, the gap is often wider and less uniform than a standard new-construction joint, so sealing work here uses hardware cloth and sheet metal cut to fit an irregular opening rather than a standard-size patch. Mouse control and rodent exclusion visits in Old Littleton spend real time on these seams specifically.

Mature trees close to the house are also worth mentioning on the call, since overhanging branches can give rodents, especially roof-capable climbers, an elevated route to the roofline that a newer, more open yard would not offer.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.

  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.

Questions about Old Littleton

Are older homes in Old Littleton more likely to have rodent problems?

They tend to have more seams and settling cracks from age and additions, so yes, inspections here often find more entry points than on newer construction.

Could my neighbor's yard be the source of activity at my house?

It is possible, especially on the closer-set lots common in this neighborhood. We ask about nearby activity during the inspection since it often points to the real source.

Do mature trees near my house increase rodent risk?

Overhanging branches can give rodents an elevated route toward the roofline, so trees close to the house are worth mentioning when you call.
